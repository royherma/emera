The best Instagram alternatives for artists

Short answerThe best Instagram alternative for artists depends on what you need: Cara is a live artist community with AI filtering, Bluesky has open feeds and portable accounts, Pixelfed is open source with chronological timelines, and Emera is a new nonprofit network with signed posts. Here is how they compare.

Where the others are the better pick

  • Cara has an art-first community that is live today, with portfolio features, job listings and AI filtering built in.
  • Bluesky is live with working apps on web, iOS and Android, over 40,000 feeds and a pledge not to train generative AI on user content.
  • Pixelfed is open source today under AGPL-3.0, connects to the wider fediverse and has chronological timelines.
  • ArtStation and Behance are established portfolio sites where many artists are already found by clients.

What artists usually want from an alternative

Most artists who look for a new home want the same few things: a place where the work is the star, a clear stance on generative AI, a feed that is not a mystery, and some control over their own account. The options below answer those in different ways, and none is the single right pick for everyone.

We built Emera, so we are listed here too. We have tried to describe every option the way its own site and docs describe it, and to say plainly where another option is the better fit today.

Cara

Cara is an app and website made for artists, run by founder Jingna Zhang with a group of volunteers. Its listing says an AI detector filters AI images out of user portfolios, and it applies NoAI tags to art as a signal to scrapers. You can customize your home feed and set up custom Explore tabs, and it has portfolio features and job listings. The iOS app is free with optional tips and supporter tiers.

If your main concern is an art community that keeps AI-generated images out, Cara is built around exactly that. For details on its source code, account export and company structure, confirm on cara.app, since we could not read those from its own pages.

Bluesky

Bluesky is run by Bluesky Social, a public benefit corporation, and the app's source code is published under the MIT license. Its FAQ says you can pick from over 40,000 feeds, switch servers without losing your username, followers or posts, and sign up without an invite. Bluesky has said it does not use user content to train generative AI. It is available on web, iOS and Android.

Bluesky is a general network rather than an art-first one, but apps built on top of it, such as Flashes, give it a photo-and-video look. Flashes is a native Bluesky photo and video app from Birdsong Apps GmbH.

Pixelfed

Pixelfed is a photo-sharing platform released under the AGPL-3.0 license. It uses the ActivityPub protocol, so it connects with Mastodon and other fediverse apps, and its timelines are chronological with no content-ranking algorithm. Official and third-party apps exist for iOS and Android. Each Pixelfed server is run by its own community, so the rules and moderation depend on where you sign up.

ArtStation and Behance

ArtStation and Behance are portfolio sites that many artists already use for hiring and showcases, not social feeds in the Instagram sense. ArtStation has added AI declarations and filters so visitors can hide AI-generated work. For Behance's current AI and training settings, confirm on behance.net.

Emera

Emera is an open-source, nonprofit network on AT Protocol, the same protocol Bluesky uses. Every post carries a signed record (C2PA content credentials) of who shared it and when it went up, and people are marked while automated accounts are labeled as automated. If you find a copy of your work elsewhere, you report it and Emera helps you file the takedown, with every step on your dashboard. You own your account and can move it with your followers to another host, and posts show a 'Why you are seeing this' reason.

Emera is new. If you need a large community today, the other options here are further along.

People also ask

What is the best Instagram alternative for artists?

It depends on what matters most to you. Cara is built for artists and filters AI images. Bluesky has open feeds and portable accounts. Pixelfed is open source with chronological timelines. Emera is a new nonprofit network that signs every post with who shared it and when.

Which alternatives are open source?

Pixelfed is released under AGPL-3.0 and Bluesky's app source code is under the MIT license. Emera's code is open source too. For Cara, confirm on cara.app.

Can I keep my followers if I move accounts?

On Bluesky and Emera, which both use AT Protocol, your account is yours and can move to another host with your followers. Pixelfed is part of the fediverse, so check pixelfed.org for how moving works on your server.

Do these apps train AI on my art?

Bluesky has said it does not use user content to train generative AI. Cara filters AI-generated images and tags art with NoAI. Read each platform's terms before you upload, since they can change.
