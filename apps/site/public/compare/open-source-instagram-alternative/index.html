Open source Instagram alternatives

Short answerThe open source Instagram alternatives are Pixelfed (AGPL-3.0, fediverse, chronological timelines), Bluesky and the photo apps built on it such as Flashes (open protocol, MIT-licensed Bluesky app), and Emera (open-source code, nonprofit, signed posts). Here is what each is like to use.

Where the others are the better pick

  • Pixelfed has a license you can read today, a federated network and chronological timelines, plus official iOS and Android apps.
  • Bluesky is live with a mature app, web, iOS and Android clients and a large feed ecosystem.
  • Flashes gives Bluesky a photo-first look with filters and portfolios on Apple devices today.

What open source means here

For a social app, open source can mean three different things: the app's code is public, the server's code is public, or the protocol underneath is an open standard anyone can build on. It helps to know which one you are getting, because it decides how much you can move, inspect and self-host.

Pixelfed

Pixelfed is a photo-sharing platform released under the AGPL-3.0 license. Its repository describes it as photo sharing for everyone, without algorithmic gatekeeping. It speaks ActivityPub, so you can follow and be followed across Mastodon and other fediverse apps. Timelines are chronological, with Local, Global and Home feeds. Official apps for iOS and Android launched in January 2025, and third-party apps exist too.

Because Pixelfed is federated, anyone can run a server, and each server sets its own rules. That is the strength of it and also the thing to check before you sign up.

Bluesky and photo apps built on it

Bluesky's app source is published under the MIT license, and AT Protocol is described as an open-source framework for building social apps. Bluesky's FAQ says you can change servers without losing your username, friends or posts. Anyone can build a feed, and its FAQ says there are over 40,000 to choose from.

Because the protocol is open, other apps can read and write the same network. Flashes, from Birdsong Apps GmbH, is a native Bluesky photo and video app for Apple devices with photo filters and portfolio building. We did not find a published Flashes codebase, so treat it as an app on an open protocol rather than an open-source app.

Emera

Emera is a nonprofit network on AT Protocol with open-source code. Each post carries a signed record (C2PA content credentials) of who shared it and when it went up, people are marked while automated accounts are labeled as automated, and reposts point back to the original post. You can pick feeds, and posts show a 'Why you are seeing this' reason. You own your account and can move it with your followers to another host.

Emera is new, so it has a small footprint compared with the options above.

People also ask

Is Pixelfed really open source?

Yes. Its repository says it is open-sourced software licensed under AGPL, and it is part of the fediverse through ActivityPub.

Is Bluesky open source?

The Bluesky social app's source code is under the MIT license, with some brand assets excluded, and AT Protocol is an open-source framework other apps can build on.

Is Flashes open source?

We could not find a published Flashes codebase. It is a native photo and video app for Bluesky from Birdsong Apps GmbH, built on the open AT Protocol.

Is Emera open source?

Yes, Emera's code is open source, and it is run as a nonprofit. It is built on AT Protocol, the same protocol Bluesky uses.
