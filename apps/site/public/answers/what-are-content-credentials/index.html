What are content credentials

Short answerContent Credentials are a signed record attached to a photo, video or file that shows where it came from and how it was edited. They follow the open C2PA standard. You can check a file at the Verify tool. They show a file's history, not whether what it shows is true.

What they are

Content Credentials are the name for the open standard from the Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity (C2PA). The C2PA site compares them to a nutrition label: a look at a file's history that anyone can read. Its steering committee includes Adobe, Amazon, BBC, Google, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI, Sony, TikTok and Truepic.

A credential is a signed bundle of facts, such as who created the file, what tool made it and what edits followed, tied to the file so tampering can be noticed.

How to verify one

  1. Go to the Content Credentials Verify tool (contentcredentials.org/verify forwards to it).
  2. Upload the original file or use a supported link.
  3. Read the result: who signed it, the tool used and the edit history, if any.

Use the original file where you can. Screenshots and files re-saved by messengers or social platforms often lose the record.

What they do not prove

A credential records provenance. It does not say that a scene is real, that a caption is honest or that the signer is trustworthy. A missing credential does not make a file fake either: most files online have none. Treat credentials as one clue next to the source and a reverse image search.

Who uses them

Support is spread across cameras, editing software, AI tools and some platforms, and it keeps changing. Check the C2PA site for the current list of members and products rather than assuming a given app supports them.

How Emera handles this

Every post on Emera carries a signed record (C2PA content credentials) of who shared it and when it went up. A Signed badge says exactly what was checked and nothing more. Emera does not say a post is true or guarantee that it is real, and it does not detect AI.

People also ask

Are Content Credentials the same as a watermark?

No. A watermark is a mark in the pixels. Content Credentials are signed metadata about the file's history. They can also be lost when a file is re-saved.

Do they stop deepfakes?

No. They help you check files that carry a credential. A fake with no credential is still possible, and a missing credential proves nothing.

Is C2PA the same thing?

C2PA is the standards group and technical specification. Content Credentials is the public name for the resulting record.

Does it cost money to check one?

The Verify tool is free to use.
